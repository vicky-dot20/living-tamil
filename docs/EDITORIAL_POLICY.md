# Editorial and scholarly policy

Living Tamil is an access layer, not an authority replacing primary sources or scholars. Its job is to make difficult material approachable while keeping provenance visible.

## Required layers

Each discovery should contain:

1. a curiosity-led hook;
2. the original Tamil excerpt;
3. a plain, modern Tamil explanation;
4. an English explanation or translation;
5. historical, literary, or cultural context;
6. why the idea still matters;
7. direct sources, rights information, and review status.

Do not silently modernize or “correct” original Tamil. Mark editorial additions, uncertain readings, composite explanations, and newly produced translations.

## Evidence standards

Prefer primary texts, critical editions, public cultural institutions, university projects, and peer-reviewed scholarship. A convenient website can help discovery but should not be the sole authority for a contested claim. Separate established evidence from interpretation and product copy.

For every borrowed asset or passage, record its creator or institution, title, URL, access date when useful, licence, and modifications. Public-domain status for an ancient work does not automatically cover a modern commentary, translation, typesetting, scan, photograph, audio performance, or database.

## Review states

- `draft`: structurally incomplete or not yet fact-checked.
- `needs-scholar-review`: sourced, but literary or linguistic interpretation needs expert review.
- `needs-archaeology-review`: material or historical interpretation needs a subject expert.
- `reviewed`: checked by a named qualified reviewer; record who reviewed it and when.

“Reviewed” must never be inferred merely because a maintainer approved the code change.

## Corrections

Corrections are welcome through an issue or pull request. Include the affected page, exact statement, proposed correction, and the strongest available source. Material errors should be corrected promptly and transparently; significant reinterpretations should be recorded in the pull-request history.

## AI use

AI can accelerate drafting and comparison, but it is not a source. No AI-produced quotation, translation, etymology, historical claim, or citation should be published without human verification against the cited material. The final wording and rights assessment remain a human contributor's responsibility.
